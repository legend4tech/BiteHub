# BiteHub: The Business Reality & Strategic Pivot
**Based on the Founder's Reflections and Market Realities**

## 1. The Catalyst: The Kene Mkparu Encounter
During a recent tech event, a conversation with Kene Mkparu (Founder of Filmhouse Cinemas) completely shifted the perspective on BiteHub. He stated a profound truth: 

> *"If you are building a product but don't understand the business—buying and selling, demand and supply, profit and loss—you are just building aesthetics."* 

He criticized hackathon pitches where founders ask for ₦15M without any realistic plan on how the business will grow to justify that funding. Their pitches were "illusions." This was a wake-up call for BiteHub. Are we building a sustainable business, or are we just writing beautiful code for an illusion?

## 2. Updated Revenue Strategy
Looking closely at BiteHub's initial model, it felt like we were running a charity. However, we recently concluded the following approach:
- **Keep 2% Vendor Commission**: We will not change the 2% vendor percentage immediately to avoid scaring off vendors early on. Once order volume hits critical mass, we can transition this to 5% or above.
- **New ₦150 Flat Transaction Fee**: To ensure we make immediate revenue, we are adding a flat **₦150 transaction/service fee** to the customer on every order.
- **Combined Fees**: To prevent "fee fatigue", the ₦150 platform fee and any gateway (Monnify) fees will be bundled together as a single "Service Fee" line item at checkout.

## 3. The "Concierge" Vendor Management Model
Top restaurants were turning off their menus due to low order volume, finding the app to be extra "stress."
- **The Pivot**: For vendors who find managing the app painful, we will act as a concierge. We tell them: *"We have an app, we know it's a pain for you to manage. When there is an order, we will call your phone, send you the money and delivery fee. You just prepare and send it to the customer."*
- If we hit high volumes and they see the value, they can choose to start managing their menus themselves.

## 4. Delivery Strategy: Vendors First, Bikes Later
- We will **not** onboard our own delivery bikes yet. We will allow vendors to handle their own delivery fulfillment for now.
- **Future Captive Fleet**: Once we raise enough capital or secure a sponsor, we will buy 2 electric bikes. Because Uli is a small geographic area, just 2 electric bikes are enough to cover the town efficiently.

## 5. Location Focus: Laser Focus on Uli
- **Pause Igbariam**: We will pause our efforts in Igbariam (or pay much less attention to it).
- We will concentrate entirely on making the model work in Uli. Small scale, high density, and achieving high volume there is the priority.

## Summary Conclusion
BiteHub is moving from an aesthetic project to a sustainable business. By keeping the vendor commission at 2% while introducing a ₦150 flat fee to the customer, we establish immediate revenue. We will baby-sit top vendors with a concierge model to secure supply, rely on vendor delivery for now with a future plan for electric bikes, and laser-focus exclusively on Uli. Aesthetics without profit is just an illusion.
